W3C TAG design-review closure latency

Snapshot: 2026-10-19T22:13:04Z. 1,197 issues; 1,149 closed; 48 open. Excludes 43 pull requests.

All-time mean: 210.8 days; median: 117.4 days; 90th percentile: 511.2 days.

Important scope effect: the bot includes charter reviews and questions. Excluding those labeled issues, the 2025 and 2026 YTD closure-year medians are both about 89 days and means are about 211 and 214 days. Thus the apparent 2025-to-2026 improvement in the all-issues average does not persist under this scope restriction. This is a label-based sensitivity analysis, not a hand-classification of every issue.

Long-term improvement is not monotonic. Among non-charter, non-question filing cohorts, the censoring-adjusted median is about 76 days for 2025 and 112 days for 2026 YTD. The latter is preliminary: 35 of 66 requests remain open, and the estimate assumes filing-date-related censoring is independent of eventual duration within the cohort. The 2026 closed-only filing-cohort mean of 54 days is not a valid estimate of the eventual mean for all 2026 requests.

Method and interpretation

  • Latency is elapsed calendar days between created_at and the current closed_at timestamp. It includes external waiting and any closed intervals before reopening; it is not staff effort or a measure of cumulative time in the open state. No individual closure-actor attribution is made.
  • Baseline matches the bot metrics page: all issues in w3ctag/design-reviews, regardless of labels. Closure does not necessarily mean a substantive review or approval: declined, withdrawn, timed-out, charter and question issues are included.
  • Closure-year results answer how long the issues closed that year took. Filing-year results answer how long a cohort of requests took. Recent closed-only filing cohorts favor quick completions; Kaplan-Meier accounts for administrative right censoring of open issues. It assumes censoring is independent of eventual duration within each cohort. Survival medians are estimates without confidence intervals, not forecasts.
  • For a filing cohort, fixed-horizon completion percentages are withheld if any still-open issue has less follow-up than that horizon. Percentiles of completed issues use linear interpolation. Kaplan-Meier uses the first time estimated survival reaches 0.5, which can differ slightly from an interpolated sample median even without censoring.
  • Annual numbers are issue-weighted, not averages of monthly averages. Sensitivity subsets use labels as observed at snapshot time; label coverage and review mix change over time. These are descriptive trends, not causal claims about TAG productivity.

Sources: review repository; bot metrics source; bot issue query. Raw API metadata and reproducible Python analysis are saved alongside this report.
